Uppsala Makerspace is a non-profit association run entirely by its members. This page explains how we are organised.

It covers:

- how you get into the space and which rules apply
- the kinds of groups
- what workshops and areas of interest are
- how certificates, tests and tutorials fit together
- how activities and events work
- how you get back money you have spent
- how the association keeps you informed

The memberships on offer and what they include are on the page [Become a member](https://www.uppsalamakerspace.se/en/become-a-member/). How the app helps you with all this is in the guide [How the app supports the association](https://tutorial.uppsalamakerspace.se/app/appOverview/).

## A non-profit association without employees

The association has no employees. Everything that gets done — keeping the workshops in order, mending machines, running courses, cleaning, looking after IT and the finances — is done by members who give their time to it.

As in every non-profit association, there are the traditional bodies. The **annual meeting** is the association's highest decision-making body. There the members elect a **board**, a **nomination committee** and **auditors**. The board leads the association between annual meetings, approves new members and is responsible for the finances. The nomination committee proposes who should be elected, and the auditors review the board's work. Who holds the elected posts, and the association's statutes, you find under [About the association](https://www.uppsalamakerspace.se/en/about/).

## Getting into the space

As a lab member you open the doors with the app when you are standing at them. Three things need to be in place first: an active lab membership, that the board has approved you, and that you have accepted the **liability undertaking**. As a basic member you get in when the space is open, on the open evenings and the Saturday courses.

The liability undertaking is the association's shared rules for everyone with their own access to the space. Among other things it says that you are responsible for your own actions, that the association's tools may not be used commercially, that you are responsible for anyone you let in, and that you clean up after yourself and leave the space in order. Anyone who does not follow the rules can be suspended. When the liability undertaking is updated, you accept it again.

If you are under 18, special rules apply; they are on the page [Become a member](https://www.uppsalamakerspace.se/en/become-a-member/).

## We run the association together

A board cannot keep a whole makerspace going on its own. That is why the day-to-day work is carried by **groups** of members. If you want to get involved, you join a group, and the group has both the responsibility and the mandate for its part: it decides how its workshop is looked after and what its budget is spent on, holds introductions and much more.

One pattern keeps coming back: *something is looked after by a group*. A workshop is run by a steering group. A part of a workshop, such as the kilns in the ceramics workshop, can be looked after by a responsibility group. What the whole association needs, such as IT or cleaning, is run by a function group.

## Groups

A group is a set of members who share a responsibility. There are three kinds of groups:

- **Steering group** — runs a workshop or an area of interest. A steering group is led jointly by its members. Example: the steering group of the ceramics workshop.
- **Responsibility group** — looks after one part of a workshop and always belongs to a steering group. Example: the kiln group within ceramics.
- **Function group** — runs something the whole association needs. Examples: IT, Cleaning, Kiosk and Social.

Every group has a **group responsible**, who keeps the group's information up to date and looks after new members.

Each group decides for itself how you get in:

- In an **open** group you join straight away.
- In other groups you **request** to join, and either the group's members or the group responsible approve your request.
- Some groups, often steering groups, **do not take requests**. There the group adds new members after you have met, using your membership number.

You need an active membership to be in a group. A group has a description, often its own rules and a Slack channel, and may have expense accounts, tutorials and activities. Only the group's own members see who is in it; others see how many they are and who the group responsible is.

## Workshops and areas of interest

A **workshop** is a room with tools and machines for a certain kind of making, such as wood, metal or ceramics. An **area of interest** gathers something around an interest, such as vinyl cutting or Saturday courses, and usually uses rooms that belong to several workshops.

You do not join a workshop or an area of interest. They describe what you can do here, and every member may use them, with the certificates some machines require.

Every workshop has its own **rules**, on safety, order and how the machines are used. They apply on top of the liability undertaking. Groups can also have rules for their work.

Every workshop and every area of interest is run by **one steering group**, often with responsibility groups under it. A steering group needs at least two members, among other things so that expenses can be handled: nobody approves their own expenses.

New workshops and areas of interest are marked *on trial* before they become established.

To get involved in a workshop, join its steering group or one of its responsibility groups.

## Certificates and tests

A **certificate** shows that you have learnt to use something safely, such as a machine or a workshop. Some machines you may only use if you hold their certificate.

A certificate can have **prerequisites**, other certificates you must hold first. It can also have a **period of validity**, after which you need to renew it.

You get a certificate in one of two ways:

- **A certifier confirms.** Certifiers are members who have been given the task of teaching and approving, for that particular certificate. It often happens on the spot in the workshop, during a walkthrough or a course event. The certifier can also give you feedback if something needs more practice.
- **You pass a knowledge test.** The test is made up of questions drawn at random from different topics, so no two attempts are the same. A certain number of mistakes are allowed, and you have a limited number of attempts. If you pass, you get the certificate at once.

## Tutorials

The groups write **tutorials** — instructions and manuals for their equipment, and descriptions of how things are done. A workshop or a group can link to its own tutorials. You find the association's tutorials at [tutorial.uppsalamakerspace.se](https://tutorial.uppsalamakerspace.se).

## Activities and events

An **activity** is something that recurs: a course, a walkthrough of a machine, an open workshop evening. An **event** is a set date when the activity takes place. There are also events that stand on their own, such as planning a new website or a visit from the municipality.

It is mostly the groups that create activities and events, for what they are responsible for. Each event can have **hosts**, who run it.

- **Sign-up** — for an event, signing up can be required or optional, or not needed at all. Sometimes a certain certificate is required to sign up.
- **Queue** — if it is full you can join the queue. When a seat becomes free, everyone in the queue is told at the same time, and whoever takes the seat first gets it.
- **Check-in** — for some events you confirm the day before that you are really coming. If you do not, you lose your seat and those in the queue get a chance to take it.
- **Attendance** — the hosts note who came.

An event can give a certificate, for example an introduction to the laser cutter. Being there is not enough on its own: a host who is a certifier confirms the certificate when you have shown that you can.

If you are interested in an activity that has no event right now, you can register your interest. Then you are told when a new event is put up.

## Expenses

Much of what is needed is bought by the members themselves: consumables, a spare part, coffee for a work day. If you are in a group that has an **expense account**, you can buy things for the association and get the money back.

- **Receipt** — you have paid yourself and get the money back to your bank account.
- **Invoice** — the association pays the supplier directly.

The group decides for itself what the money on its account is spent on, within the budget the board has set. Every expense is approved by someone other than you — nobody approves their own — and then the treasurer pays. If you are unsure whether something may be bought, ask the group before you buy.

## How we keep you informed

The association reaches you in two ways:

- **Announcements** go to all members: newsletters, roughly once a month, and other information from the board.
- **Personal messages** concern only you. It can be a welcome message when the board has approved you, a confirmation when you have paid, a reminder when your membership is about to run out, or news about an event you signed up for: that you got a seat, that a seat has become free, that it is time to check in or that the event has been cancelled.

Both kinds come by email, and you can also get them as notifications in the app.

Beyond that, we talk with each other on Slack. The board informs in #general, and you ask the board questions in #fråga-styrelsen or by email to kansliet@uppsalamakerspace.se.
